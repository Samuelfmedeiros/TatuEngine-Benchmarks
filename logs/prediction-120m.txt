-----------
Model:          120,133,920 parameters (120.1M), ternary/BitNet-style
Config:         vocab 50257, d_model 768, n_layers 12, n_heads 12
Eval device:    CPU (deterministic, no sampling for cross-entropy)
Tokenizer:      GPT-2 BPE (50257)

NEXT-TOKEN PREDICTION
---------------------
Method: teacher forcing over 512-token windows, cross-entropy against the
true next token. Top-k accuracy from argmax of the logit distribution.
No sampling, no temperature — every predicted token is the model's argmax.

CAVEAT ON THESE NUMBERS — train/eval contamination, MEASURED
------------------------------------------------------------
The evaluation corpora were drawn from the same document pools used for
pretraining, not from a held-out split. That is not an assumption — it was
measured.

Method: take the first 32 tokens of a document as its signature, then search for
that exact token sequence inside the training tensor (train_ids.pt,
2,396,160,000 tokens). A hit means the document is verbatim in the training data.

Result:
  legal PT-BR:  10 of 12 documents found in training  (83%), most 21 times
  wiki PT:      11 of 12 documents found in training  (92%), twice each

  SUPERSEDED (2026-11-14): this 12-document sample took documents from the FRONT
  of the parquet order, which is systematically more contaminated than the corpus
  as a whole. Random row-group sampling across all parquets gives:
    legal PT-BR:  129 of 200 documents in training  (64%)
    wiki PT:       53 of 120 documents in training  (44%)
  The 83%/92% figures above overstate contamination by ~19 and ~48 percentage
  points respectively. See README held-out section and CHANGELOG 2026-11-14.

Consequence: the loss figures above are an **in-distribution** measurement, not
a generalization measurement. Treating 1.6141 and 2.9815 as generalization would
be wrong — the model is being scored on text it has already seen.

What the numbers still support: relative comparison between checkpoints on the
same distribution (884K improves over 888K on legal, flat on wiki). What they do
not support: any absolute claim about unseen-text quality.

A clean run needs a held-out split — documents carved out of the parquet
collections before tokenization, hashed and excluded from train_ids.pt. That is
not what was measured here.

Roundtrip: md5 of decompressed output identical to input on both corpora.

Progress within the session:
  text gap vs xz:    6.32%  ->  1.97%
  code gap vs xz:    7.28%  ->  3.99%

Where the remaining gap sits (text corpus, 8.3 bits/token total):
  flag  9.4%  |  length 11.8%  |  distance 44.1%  |  literals 34.7%
  distance breakdown per new-distance match: slot 2.24 + log2 slot 4.47 + low bits 8.91

Methodology correction: an earlier measurement reported 14.2% gap on text. That
figure came from a synthetic corpus built by repeating documents — the match
finder located multi-kilobyte matches that do not exist in real prose. All
numbers above use real corpora.
